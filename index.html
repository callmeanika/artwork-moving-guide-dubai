How to Prepare Artwork for Moving in Dubai

Moving artwork requires more planning than packing ordinary household items. Paintings, framed pieces, prints, and decorative artwork can be damaged by pressure, moisture, heat, or careless handling during transportation.

A little preparation before moving day can make the process much easier. The goal is to protect the surface, frame, corners, and other delicate parts before the artwork leaves the property.

Inspect the Artwork Before Packing

Start by checking each piece before wrapping it.

Look for existing scratches, cracks, loose frames, damaged corners, or other marks. Taking clear photos before packing gives you a record of the artwork's condition. This is particularly useful when moving several paintings or framed pieces because it lets you keep track of each item separately.

Remove Loose or Unstable Parts

Check whether the artwork has anything that could move or come loose during transportation.

Loose frame components, hanging hardware, glass, or decorative elements may need additional protection. Do not leave small removable parts loose inside the same package.

Keep any hardware together in a labeled bag so it can be found when the artwork is ready to be displayed again.

Protect the Artwork Surface

The surface needs protection from scratches, dust, and pressure.

Use suitable protective material around the artwork rather than allowing the surface to come into direct contact with rough cardboard, wood, or other hard materials.

For framed artwork with glass, the glass also needs attention because an impact during transportation can cause cracks or breakage.

Avoid placing heavy objects directly against a painting or frame.

Protect Frames and Corners

Frames are often vulnerable at the corners.

Use appropriate padding around the edges and corners before wrapping the entire piece. This helps reduce the chance of dents, scratches, or impacts while the artwork is being carried.

If several pieces are being moved together, separate them properly instead of allowing frames to rub against each other.

Use Suitable Packing Materials

The packing method should match the size and construction of the artwork.

Small framed pieces may need less material than a large painting with a heavy frame. Larger or particularly delicate pieces may require stronger protective packaging.

Do not simply place artwork inside an oversized box with empty space around it. Movement inside the package can increase the chance of damage during transportation.

Keep Artwork Away From Heat and Moisture

Dubai's climate makes temperature and moisture worth considering when preparing artwork.

Avoid leaving paintings or framed pieces sitting in direct sunlight before loading. Artwork should also be protected from unnecessary exposure to heat and moisture during the move.

If a piece is particularly sensitive, valuable, or has delicate materials, it may need more specialized handling than an ordinary framed print.

Label and Document Each Piece

Give each artwork a simple identification label.

For example:

  • Living Room – Painting 01

You can also keep a basic record containing the artwork's description, location, and condition before packing.

Clear identification makes it easier to know which package contains which piece and where it should go at the new property.

Prepare Artwork for Transportation

Before loading begins, check that each piece is properly protected and ready to be carried.

Large framed artwork should not be placed underneath heavy boxes or other objects. Packages should also be positioned so they are not likely to shift unnecessarily during transportation.

The loading and unloading process matters just as much as the packing itself. Careful handling of large and delicate items during a move can help reduce unnecessary impacts and pressure on artwork.

Check Everything After Arrival

Once the artwork reaches the new property, inspect each piece before removing all of its protective materials.

Compare the condition with the photos taken before packing. Check the frame, corners, surface, and glass where applicable.

Allow the artwork to be placed somewhere suitable before hanging it. Avoid rushing the unpacking process simply because the rest of the move is finished.

Conclusion

Preparing artwork for a move is mainly about controlling the risks that can cause damage. Protect the surface and frame, keep loose parts organized, avoid unnecessary heat and moisture, and document the condition of each piece before transportation.
